THE OAHU ADVISORY
A Saturday Report in Verse
Saturday 5 September 2026 · 6:00 a.m. HST · Honolulu
At dawn, six bells, the isle lies calm and green,
Six hundred miles to south a giant turns;
Her eye is wide, her outer bands unseen,
And in the deep the long swell slowly churns.

The models split: some send her far to west,
While others, fewer, draw her fearful near.
The Center holds she passes, west, at best,
A hundred fifty miles from Niihau's pier.

But you have Saturday, and it is yours,
For danger sleeps until the sun goes down.
Then Sunday brings the rain and shuttered doors,
And Monday wears the storm's uneasy crown.

  So take the morning sun, the guarded bay,
  And homeward turn by four. Enjoy the day.

II.  THE PRESS, FILTERED
The mainland press cries STRIKE and BRACE and FEAR;
The island papers say she passes west.
One outlet ran both Four and Five this year
Within a single day. Trust local best.
NATIONAL
“braces for possible impacts”
“poised for dramatic turn”
“potential direct strike”
“3rd storm threat in a month”
Cat 4 and Cat 5 headlines, same day, same outlet.
LOCAL
“turn right west of Kauai County”
“near or just west of Kauai”
“big surf, then turns”
“no immediate threats”
Leads with surf and timing, not fear.
HOW TO READ THE DIFFERENCE
The mainland sells the storm; the island lives
Beneath it, and must say what is the case.
The word to watch is not what CNN gives,
But WATCH or WARNING, tied to named of place.
Neither is lying. National outlets write for readers who will not check again; local outlets write for people who must decide whether to board windows. Your signal is a watch or warning naming Oahu. Kauai getting one today is expected and is not about you.
III.  WHERE TO SWIM TODAY
Today the swell runs high on south and west;
A warning-level surf comes after dark.
So take the sheltered water, not the crest,
And leave before the evening leaves its mark.

Dangerous surf develops around Oahu tonight into Sunday, with east shores forecast to exceed High Surf Warning thresholds. Your 3–4pm exit is well ahead of it. South and west shores are already under a High Surf Advisory now.
1. Ko Olina Lagoons
West side · ~35 min from Waikiki
SAFEST
The four lagoons at Ko Olina hold
Calm water walled against the swell's assault.
Man-made crescents with rock breakwaters. The south swell cannot get in. Restrooms, showers, shade, paid lot. This is the pick if you want to actually be in the water today.
2. North Shore
Waimea Bay, Sunset, Haleiwa · ~50 min
GOOD
The North Shore sleeps in summer. Sunset lies
As flat as glass while southern swell runs high.
North-facing, and north swell season has not begun. Lowell's energy is southern, so it arrives here weakest. Best drive of the three, and Haleiwa handles a rain shower gracefully.
3. Kailua & Lanikai
Windward · ~30 min · windy today
FAIR
Kailua's reef will break the eastern chop,
But trades will gust to thirty-five. Bring hats.
Offshore reef keeps it swimmable, but strong trades mean blowing sand and chop. East shores are the ones forecast to exceed warning thresholds tonight, so this one degrades fastest. Fine until early afternoon.
AVOID TODAY
Avoid the south shore breaks and Sandy Beach;
The swell that looks like fun will not release.
Be off the sand by four. The swell will rise
To dangerous by dark. Trust not your eyes.
Open south-facing water: Ala Moana outside the reef, Waikiki on the exposed ends, Sandy Beach, Makapuu. Sandy Beach breaks directly onto sand and sends people to Queen's with neck injuries on ordinary days. Today is not an ordinary day. Locals will be out in it; that is not evidence you can be.
THE SHORT OF IT
Go west to Ko Olina, or go north;
Stay out of open water facing south.
Be dry by four, and let the night come forth
With all its noise. It will not find your house.
